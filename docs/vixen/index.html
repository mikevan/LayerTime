A fox hunt with radios. One player hides the Vixen, and the other one hunts it down.
Flash it from this page · no toolchain, no account
2 × LILYGO T-DONGLE-C5 ANDROID 8.0 OR NEWER CHROME OR EDGE ON A DESKTOP USB-C DATA CABLE

You need two T-Dongle-C5 boards. One becomes the Vixen, the dongle you hunt. It's a small Bluetooth beacon that sits in a hiding spot and does nothing else. The other becomes the Hunter. It runs the Hunter firmware, listens for the Vixen, and reports how strong its signal is. The Hunter talks to LayerScope, an Android app that turns that signal into a radar, a tone, and a step trail. Vixen is one game you can play with it. Bring two power banks: one for each dongle.

This browser can't flash. Flashing over USB needs Web Serial, and only Chrome and Edge on a desktop have it. Firefox, Safari, and every browser on a phone do not. Open this page in Chrome or Edge on a computer. You can still download LayerScope in step 3 from here.
Flashing erases the dongle. Whatever was on it before, including a Hunter's settings, is gone. Label your dongles so the Vixen doesn't end up hunting itself.

1Flash the Vixen

Plug in the dongle that will be the Vixen, then click the button and pick its port.

When it finishes, the screen goes dark. That's the point: a Vixen that glows is easy to find.

2Flash the Hunter

Unplug the Vixen. Plug in the second dongle, then click the button and pick its port.

When it finishes, the screen shows the Hunter's name, such as Hunter-C5-BSV72. That dongle is now the Hunter.

3Install LayerScope

LayerScope runs on any Android device with Android 8.0 or newer and Bluetooth: a phone, a tablet, or an Android watch. Open this page on that device and tap the button. It isn't in the Play Store, so Android will ask you to allow the install.

Download LayerScope

  2. If Android blocks it, tap Settings in the message, turn on Allow from this source, and go back.
  3. Tap Install.
  4. Open LayerScope. Allow Bluetooth and location when it asks. Android requires location permission to scan for Bluetooth devices, and the app doesn't record where you are.

How to play

The Vixen

  1. Plug the Vixen into a power bank and hide it.
  2. To check that it's running, give the BOOT button a short press. The screen lights up for 10 seconds with its name, power, and uptime, then goes dark again.

The Hunter

  1. Plug the Hunter into the other power bank.
  2. In LayerScope, tap SCAN & CONNECT.
  3. Tap HUNT VIXEN. The first time, Android asks for Physical activity permission. Allow it, because that's how the step trail counts your steps.
  4. Walk. Each step drops a dot on the trail, colored red, yellow, or green by signal strength. The tone climbs as you close in, and the side of the screen says CLOSING or OPENING.
  5. Hold a strong signal for two seconds and the screen flashes FOUND!. Tap HUNT AGAIN for another round, or STOP HUNT to quit.
It tells you hotter or colder, not which way. LayerScope doesn't use a compass, so the radar shows how close you are, never a direction. If the screen ever shows an arrow pointing at the Vixen, something is wrong. If the Vixen goes quiet for five seconds, you'll see TARGET LOST. Usually that means the power bank went to sleep.

If it won't connect

The T-Dongle-C5 uses the chip's built-in USB, so the browser can't always kick it into flashing mode by itself. If the port doesn't show up, or the install fails right away, do it by hand:

  1. Unplug the dongle.
  2. Hold the BOOT button while you plug it back in.
  3. Let go after it's plugged in, then click the install button again and pick the port.

The port shows up as USB ID 303A:1001. If nothing shows up at all, it's almost always the cable. Plenty of USB-C cables carry power and nothing else.
